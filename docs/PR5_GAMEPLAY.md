# PR #5 — Race feel, braking and personal bests

## Purpose

Make Northern Loop feel like **a racing game where the maths matters**, without changing any of the proven PR #2 physics or the hard single-screen requirement from PR #4.

### 1. Exact braking and cornering coach

In **Guided** mode, selecting an acceleration now gives two linked predictions:

1. The next move exactly as the deterministic engine calculates it.
2. A faint amber *braking line* starting at that next point, generated by repeatedly applying `(-sign(vx), -sign(vy))` until both velocity components reach zero.

A small **S** marks the predicted full-stop position. A line segment turns orange/red if that segment crosses snow according to the same continuous collision checker used by the actual race.

Important distinctions:
- An immediate move that crosses the bank is always a crash.
- A legal next position may have **no legal following move**; this is flagged as *TRAPPED NEXT TURN*.
- A full-braking forecast may cross snow even if some other steering sequence could succeed. The warning says *BRAKE EARLIER*, **not** that every possible future route will crash.
- If braking is safe, the short message gives the number of turns needed to stop. The exact component-wise stopping displacement is `sign(v) × |v| × (|v|-1)/2`.

**Standard** preserves its one-move preview, without the future braking projection. **Expert** retains no destination prediction or braking advice. Rules are identical in all modes.

### 2. More satisfying race feedback

- A fine blue momentum arrow on the skimmer shows the current velocity direction and magnitude.
- Selected movement still glides in a **mathematically straight segment**. It is not converted to curved steering or cinematic easing.
- A crash triggers a brief coral snow spray/ring, visible penalty toast, and the existing reset-to-zero-velocity logic.
- Checkpoint gates trigger a lime burst and on-track announcement.
- Finishing gets a celebratory announcement before the familiar results panel.
- Animation remains optional: reduced-motion preferences keep the game fully playable without particle movement.

### 3. Personal best and ghost challenger

- After completing a lap, the lowest score (`turns + 3×crashes`) is saved **only in browser localStorage**, never transmitted.
- A short `PB` label stays in the track header and also appears on the finishing panel.
- On a later run, the prior best lap becomes a subtle ice-blue **ghost racer**, indexed by attempted turn, including stationary turns and crashes. A compact GHOST control toggles it.
- To protect Expert's no-preview challenge, ghosts are automatically hidden in Expert mode.
- Data is shape/range checked on loading. Unavailable or blocked storage degrades gracefully to a session-only best; it never stops a race.
- No user identity, telemetry upload, third-party analytics, backend or multiplayer are involved.

### 4. Single-screen constraint preserved

The original V4 layout, camera, track geometry and controls are retained. Feedback appears **over the course**, does not take additional vertical page space, and uses no scrolling. Ghost toggle is hidden until a personal best exists. On smaller devices the redundant keyboard hint automatically disappears when the ghost control is present.

New entry: `race-v5.html`; V4 remains untouched for comparison.

## Verification

```sh
npm test
npm run check
python3 -m http.server 8000
# http://localhost:8000/race-v5.html
```

The `gameplay-v5.yml` workflow checks exact engine/braking math and local record handling, plus nine **real CSS viewport sizes** via Chrome DevTools emulation. It validates the complete on-screen cockpit, three assistance modes, tutorial/finish overlays and first movement. It also reconstructs a **20-turn legal lap** and clicks each move in the actual browser, checking all three ordered gates, the persisted personal best, the ghost toggle, Expert's ghost restriction and a separate crash with the correct +3 penalty. Once the ghost control is present, its fit is checked again at portrait and landscape phone sizes. Desktop, portrait mobile, landscape mobile and post-record ghost screenshots are retained as workflow artifacts.

## Review checklist

- **Cornering:** With speed through a bend, do the stopping-distance warnings make sense? Does a full-stop prediction actually follow valid/invalid geometry?
- **Visual hierarchy:** The actual selected move is always more prominent than its future braking guide.
- **Competition:** Can a player earn a best, race against a ghost and switch the ghost off without affecting game physics?
- **Feedback:** Are crashes and gates noticeable but brief and not disruptive?
- **Screen:** Is every essential control visible on normal laptop, tablet, projector and phones in both orientations?
- **Math:** Are the same nine accelerations, score penalty and exact line-segment collision rules still in effect?

## Not part of this pass

New tracks, AI and online multiplayer, speed-dependent traction, sound effects, powerups, track creation, accounts, classrooms and leaderboards. They should only be considered after this playable lap has been reviewed with students.
